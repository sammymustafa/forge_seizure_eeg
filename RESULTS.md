# Results

All numbers below are generated by `scripts/07_report.py` from the
cross-validation tables in `results/tables/`; none are typed by hand.

## Setup

* 23 patients (24 CHB-MIT cases; chb01 and chb21 are the same
  person), 198 annotated seizures, 973 h of recording,
  350252 10-second windows.
* Ictal prevalence 0.38% of windows.
* Shrinkage LDA, 5-fold GroupKFold over patients, thresholds fitted on
  training patients only.

## How much is lost by dropping to a wearable-sized electrode set?

Against the full 18-channel montage, as a
paired per-patient difference in AUPRC (95% bootstrap CI over patients):

* **Left temporal (2 contacts, 1 ch)**: -0.011 [-0.069, +0.045] — indistinguishable from full scalp; better in 11/23 patients.
* **Posterior-temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch)**: -0.013 [-0.069, +0.036] — indistinguishable from full scalp; better in 14/23 patients.
* **Temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch)**: -0.039 [-0.102, +0.019] — indistinguishable from full scalp; better in 11/23 patients.
* **Glasses-like (4 contacts, 2 ch)**: -0.076 [-0.154, -0.002] — worse than full scalp; better in 9/23 patients.
* **Right temporal (2 contacts, 1 ch)**: -0.090 [-0.172, -0.015] — worse than full scalp; better in 7/23 patients.

So Left temporal (2 contacts, 1 ch), Posterior-temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch), Temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch) are statistically indistinguishable from the full montage on this cohort, while Glasses-like (4 contacts, 2 ch), Right temporal (2 contacts, 1 ch) are measurably worse.

### Caveat on the comparison

At the 6 FA/h **target**, the full montage actually produced 9.6 false alarms/h on held-out patients, against 5.3–5.9 for the reduced sets: its thresholds transfer less well across patients. Part of its sensitivity edge in the table is therefore bought with extra alarms. Figure 3 plots sensitivity against the *achieved* alarm rate, and there the reduced sets are not behind.

## Side matters more than channel count

CHB-MIT has no seizure-onset-localisation labels, so the temporal-lobe question cannot be answered directly. Comparing the two single-channel detectors on the same patients is the nearest proxy:

* Left `F7-T7` minus right `F8-T8` AUPRC: +0.079 [+0.022, +0.143], left favoured in 16/23 patients.

The confidence interval excludes zero, so in this cohort *which side* a single-channel device sits on matters. That argues for a bilateral four-contact device over a one-sided two-contact one, even though the pooled two-contact numbers look adequate.

## The closest scalp analogue of a behind-the-ear device

`T7-FT9` and `FT10-T8` need FT9/FT10, which only some recordings carry, so this is a separate paired run on the subset where every montage including that one is available (339089 windows).

| Configuration | Contacts | Bipolar ch | AUPRC [95% CI] | Seizure sensitivity @ 6 FA/h target | Achieved FA/h | Median latency | Seizures detected |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full scalp (18 bipolar ch) | 19 | 18 | 0.202 [0.116–0.304] | 0.85 [0.72–0.95] | 9.6 | 17 s | 142/181 |
| Glasses-like (4 contacts, 2 ch) | 4 | 2 | 0.156 [0.085–0.239] | 0.73 [0.60–0.85] | 5.8 | 24 s | 117/181 |
| Temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch) | 4 | 2 | 0.192 [0.110–0.295] | 0.83 [0.70–0.94] | 5.6 | 20 s | 140/181 |
| Posterior-temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch) | 4 | 2 | 0.218 [0.128–0.322] | 0.82 [0.68–0.93] | 5.4 | 19 s | 137/181 |
| Lower-temporal (4 contacts, 2 ch) | 4 | 2 | 0.133 [0.079–0.199] | 0.83 [0.70–0.94] | 6.1 | 18 s | 142/181 |
| Left temporal (2 contacts, 1 ch) | 2 | 1 | 0.216 [0.129–0.320] | 0.81 [0.69–0.91] | 5.8 | 19 s | 135/181 |
| Right temporal (2 contacts, 1 ch) | 2 | 1 | 0.137 [0.083–0.198] | 0.80 [0.67–0.91] | 5.6 | 27 s | 134/181 |

Lower-temporal detects 142/181 seizures at 6.1 false alarms/h with a median latency of 18 s. That is the same seizure count as the full montage (142/181) at 9.6 FA/h, i.e. a comparable yield for fewer alarms, although its patient-mean sensitivity is slightly lower (0.83 vs 0.85) and its AUPRC is clearly lower (-0.068 [-0.146, +0.009] against full scalp, a CI that still includes zero).

## Between-patient spread

Per-patient AUPRC ranges from 0.001 to 0.794 (Figure 5). The spread between patients is much larger than the spread between electrode sets, so a patient-general wearable detector is limited far more by cross-patient variability than by electrode count.
